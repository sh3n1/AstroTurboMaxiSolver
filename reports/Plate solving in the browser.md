# Fix detection first, then tighten the solver

AstroTurboMaxiSolver's pipeline is already shaped like the best permissively licensed solvers. A tetra3-style 4-star hash plus binomial verification is the right core. The biggest remaining gains are in **star detection under a city sky**, not in pattern matching or raw compute. The clearest evidence is PiFinder's light-pollution work. On bright-sky frames, its 8-bit detector found 0–2 stars and solved **0 of 50 frames**. Adding mesh background subtraction, binning and quality gates on the full frame raised that to **44 of 50**, with **zero false solves in 1,632 live attempts** ([PiFinder PR #566](https://github.com/brickbots/PiFinder/pull/566)). The three failures seen in AstroTurboMaxiSolver's tests are edge smears along the vignetting, patches one grey level above the sky, and buildings outranking stars. Each matches a known gap against SExtractor, photutils and cedar-detect practice: no median filter over the background mesh, a noise floor below the 8-bit quantisation step, and ranking whole connected components by total flux. On the solver side, the 1e-12 acceptance threshold has no fixed meaning: it depends on the match radius, on how many hypotheses are tried, and on whether the four pattern stars are counted as matches. Tying it to those quantities, and adding a hint-driven tracking path that needs only 3–6 stars, buys both speed and safety. Sensor fusion should move from a heading-only compass offset to a full correction quaternion, timestamped at the moment of exposure, as PiFinder does. On compute, WASM SIMD is safe on every target (Safari 16.4+), while WebGPU and WASM threads are only optional later extras. Fixing the algorithm and reading the camera's Y plane directly come first.

## Three observed detector failures map onto three missing standard steps

All the reference background estimators share one design. They build a coarse mesh, take a sigma-clipped, mode-like value per cell, **median filter the mesh (typically 3x3)** to remove cells contaminated by bright objects, and only then interpolate. SExtractor clips each cell to ±3σ around its median and estimates the mode as 2.5·median − 1.5·mean, falling back to the median when the two disagree. It describes 32–512 px meshes as typical, and says the median filter also reduces ringing around bright features ([SExtractor docs](https://sextractor.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Background.html)). photutils goes further and **masks sources before estimating the background**. Its example dilates the mask with a radius of 10 px, and it marks invalid regions with a separate coverage mask ([photutils](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/user_guide/background.html)). AstroTurboMaxiSolver's 32 px block median has neither the mesh filter nor the pre-mask. One cell over a lit window or a rooftop therefore raises the local background, and bilinear blending spreads it into neighbouring cells. That is the edge-smear and halo pattern. Bilinear blending between 32 px cells also cannot follow a steep radial vignetting falloff. The fix is to divide by a radial flat stored in the mount profile, or a fitted r²/r⁴ model, before estimating the background, or else to mark high-gradient corners as low-confidence.

The quantisation patches are arithmetic, not optics. A noise floor of 0.25 DN with a 4σ threshold means a step of **exactly 1 DN** passes detection, and denoised 8-bit phone frames are full of such steps. cedar-detect handles the same problem by putting **absolute minimums of 2 and 3 DN** under its σ-based noise terms ([cedar-detect algorithm.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/smroid/cedar-detect/main/src/algorithm.rs)). AstroTurboMaxiSolver should do two things. First, raise the single-frame floor to about 0.5–1 DN; the quantisation noise of a uniform 1 DN step is 1/√12 ≈ 0.29 DN, so 0.25 is below even that. Second, require a raw peak excess of about 3 DN on single frames, or about 2 DN on a 6-frame stack. Stacking must happen in floating point. Siril sums 8-bit input into wider integers precisely because a mean taken in 8 bits "would still be 8 bits deep" ([Siril stacking docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/de/latest/_sources/preprocessing/stacking.rst.txt)). After stacking, re-estimate the noise on the Float32 stack itself, because phone denoising leaves correlated noise that does not scale by √N.

Buildings outrank stars because the detector ranks thresholded components by total flux, which rewards anything large. Production star-finders instead test whether a peak is **compact and surrounded by flat sky**. cedar-detect runs a 7-pixel row gate whose border pixels must be uniform. It then checks a 2D core against neighbour, margin and perimeter rings, which must vary by at most 1.5σ, and rejects blobs wider than **image width/100** (about 11 px here). It also rejects hot pixels whose neighbours carry less than half the centre flux, and it is designed to tolerate "the moon, streetlights, or lit foreground objects" ([cedar-detect README](https://github.com/smroid/cedar-detect); [algorithm.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/smroid/cedar-detect/main/src/algorithm.rs)). Siril smooths with a Gaussian and keeps only candidates that are the maximum within a box, which rejects bright nebula patches ([Siril Dynamic PSF](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Dynamic-PSF.html)). SEP filters with a 3x3 matched kernel by default, which lets it extract at only 1.5σ ([SEP extract API](https://sep.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/sep.extract.html)). Matched filtering is the optimal detector for a point source in white noise ([astro-ph/9310031](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/9310031)). The measured gain is modest, about **1.22–1.25× in S/N** in Herschel-ATLAS ([H-ATLAS DR1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.09615)), and lower when noise is correlated ([ACORNS-ADI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.3014)). The real value for AstroTurboMaxiSolver is structural. Peaks on a filtered image turn a lit facade into a few rejectable candidates instead of one huge component that wins on flux.

| Observed failure | Root cause | Standard remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edge smears along vignetting | Unfiltered 32 px mesh, bilinear blend over steep falloff, no pre-mask | Radial flat or fitted r²/r⁴ before background; 3x3 mesh median; clipped mode per cell; mask bright regions before the mesh |
| Patches one grey level above sky | 0.25 DN floor × 4σ = 1 DN | Floor ≥ 0.5–1 DN; absolute peak excess ≥ 3 DN (≥ 2 DN on a stack); Float32 stacking |
| Buildings and windows outrank stars | Components ranked by total flux | Gaussian matched filter + local maxima; flat-annulus compactness gate; size cap ≈ width/100; FWHM within 0.5–2× the median; rank by peak S/N |

Two more cheap rejectors fit an urban phone. The first is a **horizon mask from the current attitude** that discards pixels below about 8–10° altitude. It is my inference by analogy with how star trackers exclude the Earth limb; no phone-finder source describes it. The second is a population check: real stars share the optics' PSF, so candidates whose width is far from the median of the top 20 are suspect. Reject elongated blobs and collinear runs before building patterns. Astrometry.net found that lines of detections, such as satellite trails and diffraction spikes, produced false matches "at much higher rates than predicted" ([Lang et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.2233)). Elongation > 3 is too permissive for a still phone; about 2 is a better start. Centroid precision is not the constraint. Moment centroids reach about 0.05–0.1 px ([ISPRS](https://WWW.ISPRS.org/proceedings/XXXV/congress/comm3/papers/341.pdf)), against a plate scale of about 0.031°/px, so the soft-threshold centre of mass used by cedar-detect (weights max(I − (bg + 1σ), 0)) in a fixed window around the peak is enough. Every parameter here is a starting point to tune against catalogue overlays from verified solves, collected with `npm run phone:reports`.

## The 1e-12 threshold means different things at different match radii

Every serious solver splits the work in two. First, a cheap hypothesis comes from a 4-star asterism. Second, a strict verification counts how many projected catalogue stars land on detections and compares that with chance. tetra3 and its fork cedar-solve hash the five sorted edge ratios of a 4-star pattern into an open-addressing table and order pattern stars by distance from the centroid, so no permutations are tried ([cedar tetra3.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/smroid/cedar-solve/master/tetra3/tetra3.py); [tetra3 docs](https://tetra3.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_docs.html)). Cedar's verification takes the per-star chance probability as the number of nearby catalogue stars × match radius², computes a binomial tail with **"−2" for the degrees of freedom used by the fit**, and reports the result **multiplied by the number of database patterns**, a Bonferroni correction. The defaults are a match threshold of 1e-5 and a match radius of 0.01 of the field width ([cedar tetra3.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/smroid/cedar-solve/master/tetra3/tetra3.py)). Astrometry.net reaches the same place through a Bayes factor. Its foreground model includes a uniform "distractor" term for unmatched sources, its prior is 1e-6, and it accepts only when **K > 1e9**. Over 182,221 SDSS images it reported no false positives ([Lang et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.2233)).

A fixed 1e-12 hides how much the match radius matters. Take 25 checked stars, 30 catalogue stars in a 60°×34° field, and a binomial tail; this is my arithmetic, not a sourced figure. With a 0.1° radius, 8 chance matches have a probability of about **2e-21**. With cedar's 0.6° default radius, the same 8 matches have about **5e-9**, which fails 1e-12. Even 10 matches only reach about 4e-12. The same rule can therefore be far too strict or nearly loose, depending on a radius the threshold does not mention. Three corrections follow. First, set the match radius from measured centroid and distortion error, about 2–3 px ≈ 0.1° once a radial distortion term is fitted. Second, exclude the pattern stars from the match count, or subtract their degrees of freedom as cedar does, because they match by construction. Third, state the threshold as a per-solve false-alarm budget divided by the number of hypotheses actually tried (patterns × focal steps × hint candidates). That makes it a deliberate risk level instead of a magic number. It can then let 6 independent matches pass when the hint and radius justify it, which matters on sparse 15-star frames. No source measures false-match rates for 60° fields with about 15 stars, so a Monte Carlo on simulated and real frames is needed before loosening anything.

The sensor hint should do more than order the search. The Rust port of tetra3 has a tracking mode. Given an attitude quaternion and its uncertainty, it skips the hash, projects a catalogue cone, matches nearest neighbours, solves Wahba's problem by SVD, and verifies **without the Bonferroni division**, since no hash trials took place. It "succeeds with as few as 3 matched stars (LIS needs 4)", and it falls back to lost-in-space when tracking fails ([tetra3 crate track.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/tetra3/0.5.1/source/src/solver/track.rs)). A ±20–30° compass hint is too coarse for direct projection across a 60° field. It works well as a **filter on hash candidates**, which cuts the effective hypothesis count, and with it the false-match risk. Once a verified solve exists and only gyro motion has happened since, the tracking path applies directly. Three geometry details are worth porting from cedar: fitting focal length jointly with one radial distortion coefficient k, which costs one parameter and matters at the corners of a 60° phone field; rejecting solutions whose rotation determinant is negative, so mirror-mount frames must be flipped before solving, as the mount-profile flag already intends; and a solve timeout with exit at the first verified hit ([cedar tetra3.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/smroid/cedar-solve/master/tetra3/tetra3.py)). Database size is not a concern at this field width. BSC5 holds 9,110 stars in 285 KB ([tetra3 docs](https://tetra3.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_docs.html)), and my estimate for a multiscale 25–70° pattern table is well under 2 MB as a binary typed array.

Licence boundaries decide what may be ported. **tetra3 and cedar-solve are Apache-2.0** and can be ported with their notices kept ([esa/tetra3](https://github.com/esa/tetra3); [cedar-solve](https://github.com/smroid/cedar-solve)). Astrometry.net must be distributed under GPL-3 ([LICENSE](https://github.com/dstndstn/astrometry.net/blob/main/LICENSE)), and SEP's C core is LGPLv3 ([SEP](https://github.com/kbarbary/sep)). The most important surprise is that **cedar-detect is FSL-1.1-MIT, not Apache-2.0**. Its non-compete clause covers commercial products with "substantially similar functionality" ([cedar-detect LICENSE.md](https://github.com/smroid/cedar-detect/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). Its ideas can be reimplemented from the description, but its code should not be translated. ASTAP's licence is reported inconsistently as MPL-2.0 and GPL ([openSUSE](https://build-test-1.opensuse.org/package/show/Application:Astrophotography/ASTAP?rev=36); [AUR](https://aur.archlinux.org/packages/astap-cli)), so use its CC-BY-4.0 method page only.

## PiFinder's quaternion anchor generalises the learned compass offset

PiFinder is the only finder with an inspectable design, and it treats the solve as truth and the IMU as a dead-reckoner. At each solve it re-anchors a "slowly-drifting reference-frame quaternion" q_eq2x from the solved camera attitude and the IMU quaternion captured with that frame. It keeps a fixed IMU-to-camera rotation and a learned camera-to-eyepiece quaternion. It has no gyro-bias model; drift is absorbed at each re-anchor ([imu_dead_reckoning.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brickbots/PiFinder/main/python/PiFinder/pointing_model/imu_dead_reckoning.py)). Solves are stamped with the frame's **exposure_end**, not with the time the solve completed. The display labels each position as IMU or camera ([integrator.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brickbots/PiFinder/main/python/PiFinder/integrator.py); [PiFinder user guide](https://pifinder.readthedocs.io/en/release/user_guide.html)). Celestron says StarSense Explorer re-solves "each time the phone is repositioned" for about **0.25° accuracy**, and calls sensor-only apps "a few degrees at best" ([Celestron](https://www.celestron.com/pages/starsense-explorer-technology)). Users report phone compass and gyro errors of up to about 10° ([SC forum](https://support.simulationcurriculum.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/115009490788/comments/115005432287)), consistent with AstroTurboMaxiSolver's measured 7.5°.

AstroTurboMaxiSolver should therefore keep the OS-fused orientation as its motion source and learn a full correction quaternion, q_corr = q_solve · q_sensor(capture)⁻¹. This captures tilt and mount-geometry errors as well as heading, and is more than a heading-only offset. The sensor quaternion should come from a 2–3 s ring buffer, read at the middle of the exposure. A spacecraft-style multiplicative EKF with gyro-bias states is standard for star trackers ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20150023412/downloads/20150023412.pdf)), but it only pays off when fusing raw gyros, which the browser's OS fusion hides. A Mahony-style PI correction on q_corr is the middle ground if drift between solves proves significant ([ahrs Mahony](https://ahrs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/filters/mahony.html)). Mirror mounts fit cleanly into this model. A reflection is not a rotation, so flip x before solving and treat the mirror geometry as a fixed device-to-camera rotation that q_corr fine-tunes.

Platform details shape the inputs. DeviceOrientation Euler angles have gimbal-lock problems, and the spec itself recommends quaternions. Values are rounded to **0.1° and 0.1°/s**, and events fire only on a "significant change" ([W3C Device Orientation](https://www.w3.org/TR/orientation-event/)). A camera pointing at the zenith sits near that singularity. On Android Chrome, prefer `AbsoluteOrientationSensor`, which gives a quaternion at up to 60 Hz and supports `referenceFrame: 'screen'` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AbsoluteOrientationSensor); [Chrome Generic Sensor](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/generic-sensor)). On iOS, alpha is relative to the start-up heading ([W3C alpha note](https://www.w3.org/2008/geolocation/wiki/images/e/e0/Device_Orientation_%27alpha%27_Calibration-_Implementation_Status_and_Challenges.pdf)). The absolute heading must come from `webkitCompassHeading`, which is magnetic and accurate to about 10° ([W3C list](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-geolocation/2011Jul/0014.html)), so iOS will lean harder on solves. PiFinder's 0.06° motion threshold cannot be reproduced from rounded angles. A windowed test (under about 0.1–0.2° over 0.5 s) plus a short settling delay is more realistic. My blur budget is about 0.031°/px, which allows roughly **0.19°/s at 1/3 s and 0.5°/s at 1/8 s** for 2 px of blur. The 1/3 s case is near the sensor's precision limit, so it also needs an image-based check, such as rejecting stacks with elongated stars.

## WASM SIMD is universal; WebGPU and threads are optional extras

Browser compute should come after the algorithmic fixes. The current 150–300 ms per frame on desktop leaves plenty of headroom. cedar-detect runs at **under 10 ms per megapixel on a Raspberry Pi 4B** in native Rust ([cedar-detect README](https://github.com/smroid/cedar-detect)). The cheapest gains need no new dependency. With 8-bit data, a 256-bin histogram per block gives an exact median and MAD in linear time, with no sort. Connected components should start only from pixels above the threshold. Detection can run on a 2x2-binned frame while centroids are computed at full resolution, as cedar-detect does. Acquisition is the next win. On Chrome and Safari 18+, `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` delivers `VideoFrame`s inside the worker ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaStreamTrackProcessor); [Mozilla WebRTC blog](https://blog.mozilla.org/webrtc/unbundling-mediastreamtrackprocessor-and-videotrackgenerator/)). `copyTo()` without a `format` returns the native layout, usually NV12 or I420, whose Y plane is already the greyscale image, so no canvas and no RGBA conversion are needed ([MDN copyTo](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/VideoFrame/copyTo)). iOS 17 keeps the canvas path.

| Technique | Android Chrome | iOS Safari | Cost | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WASM fixed-width SIMD | 91+ | 16.4+ | Toolchain only | Adopt for background, filter and threshold kernels |
| WASM threads | 74+ | 14.1+ | COOP/COEP cross-origin isolation | Later, after HTTPS hosting |
| WebGPU | 121+, Android 12+, Qualcomm/ARM GPUs | iOS 26+ only | Second code path, readback cost unknown | Optional pre-pass, behind feature detection |
| MediaStreamTrackProcessor + Y plane | Yes | 18+ | Small | Adopt with canvas fallback |

Support data comes from [WebAssembly features.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebAssembly/website/main/features.json) and [web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers). Published speedups are desktop-only and from 2019–2020. OpenCV.js kernels gained about **9.5× from SIMD and about 30× from SIMD plus threads** over scalar WASM ([GSoC report](https://gist.github.com/Wenzhao-Xiang/8636e239c20e0eda839833f9d363828e)), and TensorFlow.js gained 1.7–4.5× from SIMD ([TensorFlow blog](https://blog.tensorflow.org/2020/09/supercharging-tensorflowjs-webassembly.html)). Branch-heavy medians and labelling will gain far less over tight typed-array TypeScript. My working estimate is 1.5–3×, which needs measuring on the Magic5 Pro. Threads need SharedArrayBuffer and therefore COOP/COEP headers, and `credentialless` is reported as unreliable on Safari ([Shen.ai](https://developer.shen.ai/getting-started/web-troubleshooting)). WebGPU suits per-pixel subtraction and per-block histograms, but connected components needs many dispatches. There are **no published mobile readback benchmarks**, and iOS 17–18 has no WebGPU at all, so it can only ever be an accelerator over a complete CPU path. OpenCV.js is Apache-2.0 but weighs about 6.4 MB of WASM ([OpenCV PR #23344](https://github.com/opencv/opencv/pull/23344/files/6c763e1ea509404465e79e0556a0d02b7921b8e3)), which is too heavy for a few kernels. A small Rust or C kernel is the better choice if WASM is adopted, and that is a dependency decision to raise with the user first.

## Prioritised actions

| # | Action | Why | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pre-mask bright regions (≥ 240 DN or large > 20σ components, dilated 10–20 px) and below-horizon pixels; exclude them from background and detection | Sharp foreground breaks every mesh estimator; photutils masks first | Low |
| 2 | Add a 3x3 median filter over the background mesh, sigma-clipped mode per cell, and radial flat correction for vignetting | Fixes edge smears and halos | Low |
| 3 | Raise the noise floor to ≥ 0.5–1 DN and require ≥ 3 DN peak excess; stack in Float32 | Fixes quantisation patches | Low |
| 4 | Detect local maxima on a Gaussian-filtered image, then apply a flat-annulus compactness gate, a width/100 size cap, a FWHM consistency check and rank by peak S/N | Stops buildings outranking stars | Medium |
| 5 | Redefine acceptance: radius from measured error, pattern stars excluded, threshold = budget / hypotheses tried; validate by Monte Carlo | Makes 1e-12 a deliberate false-alarm rate | Medium |
| 6 | Add a tracking solve (project, nearest neighbour, SVD, verify without Bonferroni) after a verified solve; use the compass hint as a hash-candidate filter | Solves with 3–6 stars, faster and safer | Medium |
| 7 | Fit focal length with radial k per device; reject det < 0 | Corner stars stay in the match radius | Low |
| 8 | Replace the heading offset with a full q_corr quaternion, a timestamped sensor ring buffer and a "sensor estimate" vs "solved" status | Matches PiFinder; honours "never show unverified as correct" | Medium |
| 9 | Use AbsoluteOrientationSensor on Android; webkitCompassHeading + WMM on iOS | Avoids gimbal lock and handles iOS's relative alpha | Low |

## Conclusion

The research turns an apparent compute or solver problem into a measurement problem. Each change in the detector and verifier is cheap. The open question is calibration: centroid error, lens distortion, a real false-match rate for 60° fields with about 15 stars, and the actual Y-plane format and denoising each phone delivers. None of these is published anywhere, so the device-report pipeline and a Monte Carlo harness on Valencia frames become the critical infrastructure, more valuable than any port.

The licence landscape also narrows the options more than expected. The obvious "city-tolerant detector" to borrow, cedar-detect, is under a non-compete licence. AstroTurboMaxiSolver's detector must therefore be its own reimplementation of published ideas, while the solver can lean openly on Apache-2.0 tetra3 and cedar-solve. That split is an advantage. It concentrates original engineering in detection, where the urban-phone problem is unsolved and where a well-tuned gate stack would differentiate the app.
