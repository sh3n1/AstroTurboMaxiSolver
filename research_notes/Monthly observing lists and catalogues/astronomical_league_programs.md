# Astronomical League observing programs as candidate built-in object lists

Research date: 2026-11-11. Budget-limited pass (about 16 tool calls). Many League pages and PDFs could not be fetched or text-extracted (PDF text extraction failed for the 2026 "Getting Started" deck). Anything not backed by a fetched source is in the **Gaps** sections, marked "unverified (prior knowledge)". Treat those as leads to check, not facts.

## 1. Which programs exist, and what each list contains (size, object types, catalogues, hemisphere, aperture, organisation)

### Takeaway
The League runs about 63 to 70+ observing programs and awards. Only some have a fixed list of deep-sky objects or stars. The best candidates not already in the app are Urban, Deep Sky Binocular, Binocular Messier, Double Star, Carbon Star, Open/Globular Cluster, Planetary Nebula, Bright Nebula, Dark Nebula, Two in the View, Stellar Evolution and Southern Skies Binocular. Many programs (Lunar, Galileo, Comet, Asteroid, Analemma, Citizen Science, Constellation Hunter) have no fixed list of catalogue objects, or none that maps to a sky position.

### Cited Findings

  - The index is split into alphabetical sub-pages. Only the A to C page was retrieved, so D to Z programs (Dark Nebula, Deep Sky Binocular, Double Star, Flat Galaxy, Galaxy Groups and Clusters, Galileo, Globular Cluster, Herschel 400, Herschel II, Local Galaxy Group, Lunar, Lunar II, Messier, Open Cluster, Planetary Nebula, Sky Puppy, Southern Sky, Southern Skies Binocular, Stellar Evolution, Two in the View, Universe Sampler, Urban, Variable Star, and others) were not checked on the 2026 index. — [AL Observing Programs](https://www.astroleague.org/observing-programs/)
  - Program URLs mix old static paths (`/al/obsclubs/...`) and new WordPress slugs. Both Constellation Hunter entries point to the same URL. — [AL Observing Programs](https://www.astroleague.org/observing-programs/)
  - **Bennett Observing Program** and **Beyond Polaris** appear on the current index. Bennett (the southern Bennett catalogue of comet-like objects) is a possible southern list. — [AL Observing Programs](https://www.astroleague.org/observing-programs/)
- "Today, with 63 different Observing Programs and Observing Awards" (RASC Observer's Handbook 2022, US edition, written by the League's Observing Program Director). Each program "has a list of objects to pursue", the top level is "often 100" objects, and "the lists are designed to take you through the different seasons". — [RASC OH 2022 AL article](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Astronomical-League_Observing_Programs.pdf)
- A secondary source (Astronomy magazine) speaks of "70-plus observing programs" listed alphabetically on the League site. — [Astronomy.com, Mar 2021](https://www.astronomy.com/observing/backyard-observing-lists/) (seen as a search snippet only)

**Program groupings (from the League's Observing Program Director, 2022)**
- Introductory programs: Constellation Hunter, Messier, Binocular Messier, Lunar, Solar System, plus a choice of Galileo, Sketching, Sky Puppy (youth aged 10 and under), Youth Astronomer (17 and under), Beyond Polaris (adult introduction), Two in the View, Universe Sampler or Urban. — [RASC OH 2022](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Astronomical-League_Observing_Programs.pdf)
- Master Observer requires Messier, Binocular Messier, Lunar, Double Star and Herschel 400. — [RASC OH 2022](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Astronomical-League_Observing_Programs.pdf)
- "Advanced" stellar and deep-sky programs: Asterisms, Asteroids, Bright Nebulae, Carbon Star, Comets, Dark Nebulae, Earth Orbiting Satellites, Globular Clusters, Meteors, Open Clusters, Solar, Planetary Nebulae and "one of the Galaxy-based Observing Programs". — [RASC OH 2022](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Astronomical-League_Observing_Programs.pdf)
- Higher levels name Lunar II, Sketching, Sunspotters, Herschel II, Hydrogen Alpha Solar, Stellar Evolution, Variable Star, Dark Sky Advocate, Target NEO and Radio Astronomy. — [RASC OH 2022](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Astronomical-League_Observing_Programs.pdf)
- Binocular programs (Binocular Master Observer, any 8 of 9): Binocular Double Star, Advanced Binocular Double Star, Binocular Messier, Binocular Variable Star, Deep Sky Binocular, Galileo Binocular, Lunar Binocular Certificate, Solar System Binocular Certificate, Southern Skies Binocular. — [RASC OH 2022](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Astronomical-League_Observing_Programs.pdf)

**Programs checked individually**
- **Bright Nebula:** 150 bright nebulae. Needs an 8-inch or larger telescope; UHC/OIII filters recommended. Designed so both northern and southern observers can complete it. Uses Lynds' Catalog of Bright Nebulae for brightness ratings and Uranometria 2000 coordinates. The list is published as both a **PDF and an Excel spreadsheet**: `https://www.astroleague.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/BN-List.pdf` and `.../BN-List.xlsx`. The page did not show the catalogues used (NGC/IC/Sh2/vdB) or the list's ordering. — [AL Bright Nebula](https://www.astroleague.org/bright-nebula-observing-program)
- **Carbon Star:** the current page is only a store notice: the manual is sold through League Sales. No free list was linked from that page. — [AL Carbon Star](https://www.astroleague.org/carbon-star-observing-club)
- **Urban:** observe 100 objects from the Urban list under light-polluted skies, defined as skies "where one cannot see the Milky Way". There are two lists, one of deep-sky objects and one of double and variable stars. All objects are in RA order. The deep-sky list includes open clusters, globulars, planetary nebulae and galaxies (e.g. NGC 3242 planetary nebula, M41 open cluster). The list is on its own web page, `astroleague.org/urban-program-deep-sky-objects-list/`. — [AL Urban](https://www.astroleague.org/urban-observing-program/); [AL Urban DSO list](https://www.astroleague.org/urban-program-deep-sky-objects-list/) (both seen as search excerpts, not fetched in full)
- **Stellar Evolution:** 100 objects ("All 100 objects in Astroleague Stellar Evolution Club"). — [SkySafari repository](https://go.skysafariastronomy.com/repositories/index.php?dir=skylist%2FAstronomical+League&theme=onyx)
- **Two in the View:** a list of pairs or groups. The third-party skylist holds only the brightest object of each group, 109 entries. — [SkySafari repository](https://go.skysafariastronomy.com/repositories/index.php?dir=skylist%2FAstronomical+League&theme=onyx)

### Inferences
- Programs whose list is mostly Messier/NGC/IC (Urban, Binocular Messier, Bright Nebula, Globular Cluster, Planetary Nebula, Two in the View, Galaxy Groups) could be built from data the app already has, OpenNGC plus Messier.
- Arp, Flat Galaxy, Local Group and Galaxy Groups lists are faint "large telescope" programs. They fit the app's city-sky, small-scope users less well than Urban, Deep Sky Binocular and Double Star.
- Programs with no fixed list of sky positions (Lunar features, Galileo, Comet, Asteroid, Analemma, Solar, Meteors, Satellites, Citizen Science, Radio, Sketching, Outreach) cannot be stored as designation lists. Lunar would need a separate Moon-feature dataset and a Moon view.

## 2. Where the authoritative lists live, and whether machine-readable lists exist

### Cited Findings
- Bright Nebula has an official League **XLSX** list (`/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/BN-List.xlsx`) and a PDF list. — [AL Bright Nebula](https://www.astroleague.org/bright-nebula-observing-program)
- Urban has its deep-sky list as an HTML page on the League site. — [AL Urban DSO list](https://www.astroleague.org/urban-program-deep-sky-objects-list/)
- Some manuals (e.g. Carbon Star) are sold in print through the League store, not posted free. — [AL Carbon Star](https://www.astroleague.org/carbon-star-observing-club)
- SkySafari's own resource repository (Simulation Curriculum) hosts community **.skylist** files in a folder "skylist/Astronomical League": AL Bright Nebula, AL Carbon Star List, AL Deep Sky Binocular List, AL Double Star List, AL Globular Clusters, AL Herschel II, AL Planetary Nebula Club, AL Sketching, AL Stellar Evolution (100 objects), AL Two In The View (109), AL Urban Club, plus a user-uploaded "AL Observing Stellar Evolution" folder. No README or licence text. — [SkySafari repository](https://go.skysafariastronomy.com/repositories/index.php?dir=skylist%2FAstronomical+League&theme=onyx)
- An observer's site offers a Google Drive folder of SkySafari skylists "I've made or collected for Astronomical League Observing Programs", with no list of programs and no permission statement. — [Steve Boerner observing logs](https://sites.google.com/view/observing-logs-new/home)
- Stargazers Lounge users share .skylist files including "AL Dark Nebulae" lists. — [Stargazers Lounge](https://stargazerslounge.com/topic/274840-sky-safari-observing-lists) (search snippet)
- The Urban list is also copied on a third-party site, astromax.org. — [astromax Urban list](https://www.astromax.org/aa02002.html) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- The cleanest sources are the League's own XLSX or HTML lists, where they exist. Community skylists are convenient but have no stated licence and may lag behind revised lists.

### Gaps
- It was not checked program by program which lists have an official XLSX or CSV. The `/wp-content/uploads/` path pattern suggests other programs have one too (unverified).
- No Stellarium-format AL lists were found in this pass.

## 3. Copyright and terms of use; whether commercial apps ship AL lists

### Takeaway
League pages carry "© All Rights Reserved" and no reuse terms. The League's manual style guide asks authors to assign copyright to the League and uses the clause "No part of this publication may be reproduced ... without written permission", except personal use. Nothing found addresses software or apps, and no commercial app was found to ship AL lists under a stated arrangement. **Licensing is uncertain. Ask the League for written permission before shipping AL-branded lists.**

### Cited Findings
- Every fetched League page shows "2026 © All Rights Reserved" and gives no separate terms of use. — [AL Observing Programs](https://www.astroleague.org/observing-programs/); [AL Bright Nebula](https://www.astroleague.org/bright-nebula-observing-program)
- The League's observing-manual style guide: authors are "asked to assign the copyright of your manual to the Astronomical League". The League "must have written permission to use material that is owned or copyrighted by others", and an email can suffice. The standard notice: "No part of this publication may be reproduced or utilized in any form or by any means without written permission", with "limited permission ... for the downloading, reproducing, and/or printing of the material for personal use". — [AL Observing Manual Style Manual](https://www.astroleague.org/files/u220/AL%20White%20Paper%20-%20Observing%20Manual%20Style%20Manual.pdf) (search summary; PDF not fetched in full)
- The League says it does not officially endorse outside resources, including software. — [AL EOSOP resources](https://www.astroleague.org/?p=8663) (search snippet)
- SkySafari Plus and Pro ship generic "best of" lists. User observing lists start empty ("My Favorites"), and AL users import .skylist files themselves. No official AL list in SkySafari was found. — [SkySafari Observing Lists Help](https://support.simulationcurriculum.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015943028-Observing-Lists-Help); [SkySafari community post](https://support.simulationcurriculum.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/115009176068/comments/115005126187)
- SkySafari's vendor hosts user-uploaded AL skylists in its public repository, with no licence notice. — [SkySafari repository](https://go.skysafariastronomy.com/repositories/index.php?dir=skylist%2FAstronomical+League&theme=onyx)

### Inferences
- A list of designations (e.g. "NGC 3242, M41, ...") is mostly factual data. In the US, a bare list of facts may get little or no copyright protection. A curated selection could still be protected as a compilation, and the EU (where the user is, in Valencia) has a database right. This is not legal advice.
- Lower-risk options:
  1. Ask the League for written permission by email; their own guide says email can suffice.
  2. Ship our own curated lists inspired by program themes (e.g. "City targets", "Carbon stars", "Binocular doubles") without the League name or exact selection.
  3. Use import: let users load a list they fetched themselves (CSV/skylist), so the app ships no AL data.
- Avoid the League name and logo in the app unless permission is given ("Astronomical League" is the organisation's name and its program names are part of its identity).

### Gaps
- No League statement on software or app reuse of object lists was found. No commercial app (SkySafari, SkyTools, Stellarium, Astronomy Tools) was confirmed to ship AL lists, nor any licence arrangement. SkyTools' observing-list features were not checked.
- No Cloudy Nights thread quoting the League's terms was found in this pass.

## 4. Designations that need data beyond OpenNGC (NGC/IC) + Messier + Caldwell + WDS bright doubles + named stars

### Takeaway
Urban, Binocular Messier, Herschel-based lists, Globular, Planetary Nebula, Bright Nebula and Two in the View are mostly NGC/IC/Messier and could reuse OpenNGC (which also cross-identifies some extra catalogues). Carbon Star, Variable Star, Dark Nebula, Open Cluster, Deep Sky Binocular, Asterism, Arp, Flat Galaxy, Galaxy Groups, Local Group and Lunar need extra position data.

### Cited Findings
- Bright Nebula relies on Lynds' Catalog of Bright Nebulae (LBN) ratings and Uranometria coordinates, which suggests some entries may lack NGC/IC numbers (e.g. Sharpless or vdB objects; unconfirmed). — [AL Bright Nebula](https://www.astroleague.org/bright-nebula-observing-program)
- The Urban deep-sky list uses Messier and NGC designations (e.g. M41, NGC 3242). Its second list is double and variable stars. — [AL Urban DSO list](https://www.astroleague.org/urban-program-deep-sky-objects-list/)
- Community AL skylists exist for Dark Nebulae, alongside separate Melotte and Collinder lists, showing those catalogues are wanted by AL observers. — [Stargazers Lounge](https://stargazerslounge.com/topic/274840-sky-safari-observing-lists)

### Inferences (data needs per program, based on the catalogue types each theme implies; check against the actual lists)
| Program | Likely extra data needed |
| --- | --- |
| Urban (DSO list) | Little. Messier/NGC are in OpenNGC. The double/variable list needs WDS pairs plus GCVS variables |
| Binocular Messier, Herschel 400/II, Globular | None beyond Messier/NGC/IC (Palomar globulars, if present, need positions) |
| Planetary Nebula | Possibly Abell PN, PK, Jones-Emberson or Minkowski numbers |
| Bright Nebula | Possibly Sharpless (Sh2), vdB, LBN, Ced |
| Dark Nebula | Barnard (B) and Lynds (LDN) dark nebula positions |
| Open Cluster, Deep Sky Binocular, Asterism | Collinder, Stock, Melotte, Trumpler, King, Berkeley; informal asterisms need their own coordinates |
| Carbon Star, Variable Star, Binocular Variable | Star positions with GCVS names or HD/SAO/HIP numbers |
| Double Star, Binocular Double Star | WDS discoverer codes (STF, STT, etc.); probably covered if the app's WDS bright-double subset is large enough |
| Arp North/South, Flat Galaxy, Galaxy Groups, Local Group | Arp, Arp-Madore, FGC/RFGC, UGC, PGC, Hickson, Abell galaxy clusters, DDO |
| Lunar, Lunar II | Lunar feature names with selenographic coordinates; needs a Moon view, not a sky position |

- Licences to check for those extra datasets: GCVS, WDS, Sharpless, Barnard/Lynds via VizieR (usually free for science use; commercial use must be checked per catalogue); HyperLEDA/PGC; the Arp catalogue. The app's licence rules forbid copyleft. — no source fetched; this is a to-do.

- Exact designations in each AL list were not read. The table above is inferred from program themes and prior knowledge, not from the lists.
- OpenNGC's coverage of extra identifiers (e.g. its "addendum" with some Melotte/Collinder/Barnard entries) was not checked in this pass.
