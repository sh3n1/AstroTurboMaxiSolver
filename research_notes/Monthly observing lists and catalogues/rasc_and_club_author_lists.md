# RASC programs and other club, society and author-made deep-sky observing lists

Scope: candidate built-in lists for a closed-source PWA that already ships Messier, Caldwell, Herschel 400, Herschel II, SAC 110 Best NGC and RASC Finest NGC. The app stores designations only. Nothing here is legal advice. Research done 2026-11-11 with a limited number of searches, so several items below are gaps and are marked as such.

## RASC observing programs: contents, counts, organisation, publication and copyright

### Takeaway
Besides Messier and Finest NGC, the RASC runs Explore the Universe (novice, 24 deep-sky objects plus 20 doubles, mostly Messier objects and bright named asterisms), Deep-Sky Gems (154 objects from David Levy, by season), the Deep-Sky Challenge (45 "fringe" objects for 25–30 cm or larger scopes), a Double Stars program (110 pairs), and two lunar programs. All are published free on rasc.ca as PDF, Excel and SkySafari/SkyTools files. Every page and PDF says "© … Royal Astronomical Society of Canada. All rights reserved." I found no licence that allows reuse in software.

### Cited Findings
**Program index and copyright**
- The RASC "Observing" menu lists Observing Programs (`/certificate-programs`), the Astroimaging Certificate, Special Observing Projects (Sirius B Challenge, asteroids, comets, variable stars), AstroSketchers and a World Asterisms Project. Site footer: "© Copyright 2023 The Royal Astronomical Society of Canada. All rights reserved." No other terms of use are stated. — [RASC Observing](https://www.rasc.ca/observing); [RASC Observing Programs 2019](https://www.rasc.ca/node/54944)
- Certificates awarded in 2019: Explore the Universe 10, Explore the Moon (Telescope) 4, Messier 5, Finest NGC 2, Isabel Williamson Lunar 1. 27 centres were Local Certification Centres for Explore the Universe. — [RASC Observing Programs 2019](https://www.rasc.ca/node/54944)
- The Deep-Sky Challenge page links these related RASC programs: Explore the Universe, Explore the Moon, Messier Catalogue, Finest NGC Objects, Isabel Williamson Lunar Observing Program, Deep-Sky Gems and Double Stars. — [RASC Deep-Sky Challenge](https://www.rasc.ca/node/47252)

**Explore the Universe (EtU)**
- A novice program, open to members and non-members, that "can be completed entirely using binoculars". It has sections for constellations and bright stars (12 of 24), the Moon (16 of 32: maria 6 of 12, craters 6 of 12), the Solar System (5 of 10), deep-sky objects (12 of 24) and double stars (10 of 20). This is the 6th edition, 2018 May 20, by the RASC Observing Committee. Each page carries "© 2018 The Royal Astronomical Society of Canada", and the log forms say "© 2017 Royal Astronomical Society of Canada. All Rights Reserved." The blank log pages "may be photocopied". — [EtU workbook PDF](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/EtUworkbookComplete%2002%2013%202020%20for%20web.pdf)
- The 24 deep-sky objects, extracted from the workbook text (some may be "same field" companions, not separate entries): M4, M5, M8, M11, M13, M16 or M17, M18, M22, M23, M24 (Sagittarius Star Cloud, "mislabelled as NGC 6603", with IC 4725/M25), M31, M35, M36, M37, M38, M42 (with NGC 1981), M44, M45, M46, M47, the Double Cluster NGC 869 & 884, Melotte 111 (Coma Star Cluster), Melotte 20 (Alpha Persei Group), Melotte 25 (Hyades), Kemble's Cascade, and Brocchi's Cluster (Collinder 399). — [EtU workbook PDF](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/EtUworkbookComplete%2002%2013%202020%20for%20web.pdf)
- EtU double stars are given by Bayer/Flamsteed names, for example Zeta & 80 UMa (Mizar and Alcor), Alpha² Lib, Mu¹ Boo, Nu¹ Dra, Omicron¹ Cyg, Beta Cyg (Albireo), Theta¹ Tau, Delta Cep, Epsilon Lyr and Zeta Lyr (Vega area), and Alpha² Cap (Algedi). Variable stars Mira (Omicron Ceti) and Algol (Beta Persei) also appear. — [EtU workbook PDF](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/EtUworkbookComplete%2002%2013%202020%20for%20web.pdf)
- The full official list is also in the printed "Explore the Universe Guide" (2nd ed.), sold by the RASC. — [RASC Explore the Universe](https://rasc.ca/node/47112)

**Deep-Sky Gems (DSG)**
- 154 objects shared by David Levy from his personal list, first published in the RASC Observer's Handbook in 2008. Open to RASC members only, and objects can be observed in any order. — [About the Deep-Sky Gems](https://rasc.ca/node/46938)
- The standalone list (6 pages, Nov 2022) is organised as The Autumn Sky, The Winter Sky, The Spring Sky (parts 1–3) and The Summer Sky. Each page footer reads "Copyright © 2023 Royal Astronomical Society of Canada". The columns are Levy number, NGC, RA, Dec, magnitude, size, Uranometria pages, remarks and a "Quick Note". I counted 154 rows. — [Deep-Sky Gems Final 221107 PDF](https://www.rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Deep-Sky_Gems_Final_221107.pdf)
- Most entries are NGC. The non-NGC designations I extracted: IC 1830, IC 2194/2196/2197 (Castor Cluster galaxies, combined), IC 2367, UGC 5373 (Sextans B), UGC 6253, Arp 321 ("Larry, Moe & Curly"), Tombaugh 5, the double quasar 0957+561 A/B, and three Levy asterisms with no other catalogue number: Levy 384 (Puppis, "contains star TYC 05438-2084 1"), Levy 401 (Churchill's "V", near HR 3612 / 38 Lyn) and Levy 403. — [Deep-Sky Gems Final 221107 PDF](https://www.rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Deep-Sky_Gems_Final_221107.pdf)
- A SkySafari supplement exists, and the RASC notes that targets have been added and removed over the years. The RASC Toronto Centre hosts seasonal JSON lists (for example "Deep Sky Gems - Summer v2"). — [DSG SkySafari supplement](https://www.rasc.ca/sites/default/files/DSG%20SkySafari%20supplement%20221109.pdf); [RASC Toronto DSG Summer JSON](https://rascto.ca/sites/default/files/Deep%20Sky%20Gems%20-%20Summer%20v2.json)

**Deep-Sky Challenge (DSC)**
- Compiled by Alan Dyer and Alister Ling in 2001. Its 45 objects go "beyond the Messier Catalogue and the Finest NGC objects", with no overlap: 2 open clusters (e.g. NGC 609, NGC 6791), 4 globular clusters (including Palomar 11), 13 emission/reflection nebulae (including the California Nebula and Barnard's Loop), 2 dark nebulae (B33 Horsehead, B72 Snake), 8 planetary nebulae (including Jones 1), 1 supernova remnant, and 15 galaxies, galaxy clusters and quasars. — [RASC Deep-Sky Challenge](https://www.rasc.ca/node/47252)
- A 30 cm telescope is recommended (25 cm under good skies), a few objects need 50 cm, and a nebula filter is needed for many. RASC members only, any season. Lists are offered as Excel, PDF and SkySafari plain text, plus a SkyTools 4 list. — [RASC Deep-Sky Challenge](https://www.rasc.ca/node/47252); [DSC SkySafari supplemental PDF](https://www.rasc.ca/sites/default/files/DSC%20SkySafari%20supplemental%20230729y.pdf)

**Double Stars program**
- 110 pairs, all reachable with at least 90 mm aperture, aimed at intermediate visual observers. Version 1.0 launched October 2020. Members who observe all 110 can apply for a certificate. The checklist comes as PDF or Excel and deliberately leaves out separation, PA and colours. A supplementary list leaves out pairs under 1.0″ and stars fainter than mag 13.0. — [RASC Double Stars](https://rasc.ca/node/55363); [RASC DS main checklist](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/RASC%20DS%20main%20checklist%20210411.pdf); [RASC DS supplement](https://www.rasc.ca/sites/default/files/RASC%20DS%20supplement%20210327.pdf)

**Lunar programs**
- The Isabel Williamson Lunar Observing Program requires 4 general-view observations plus 135 lunar features on the Main Observing List, with 12 optional libration targets. Certificate for RASC members. The current guide is the Revised Third Edition, September 2015. The Moon wiki says all features are reachable with 80 mm and up. — [RASC IWLOP](https://rasc.ca/node/47179); [IWLOP 2015 PDF](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/IWLOP2015.pdf); [the-moon.us wiki](https://the-moon.us/wiki/Isabel_Williamson_Lunar_Observing_Program)
- Explore the Moon has binocular and telescope versions (EtM Telescope V4). — [EtM Telescope V4 PDF](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/EtM_Telescope_V4_1.pdf)

**Other RASC material**
- The RASC also publishes "Backyard observing lists" (PDF), which was not examined in detail. — [RASC Backyard observing lists](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Backyard_observing_lists.pdf)

### Inferences
- The best RASC additions are **Deep-Sky Gems** (154, seasonal, matching the app's existing season split) and **Explore the Universe deep-sky + doubles** (a binocular and beginner list in a city sky, and nearly all of it is already resolvable in the app's data). The **Deep-Sky Challenge** is aimed at 30 cm+ dark-sky observers, a poor fit for a phone finder whose reference case is a 17.5 mag/arcsec² city sky. The **Double Stars 110** suits city observing well, but its designations need a double-star source (WDS/STF numbers or Bayer names).
- The lunar programs are lists of named lunar features, not sky positions. They would need a lunar feature gazetteer (e.g. IAU/USGS nomenclature) and a different UI, so they are out of scope for a sky finder.
- Copyright: the RASC reserves all rights and grants no software licence. The selections were made by named people (Levy, Dyer and Ling), which is exactly the "original selection" that compilation copyright can protect. The safest route is to email the RASC Observing Committee (observing@rasc.ca, named on its pages) for written permission to ship designation lists with credit. The app already ships Finest NGC, so check whether that permission was already sought.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full 45-object DSC designation list from a primary source. The SEDS mirror (messier.lamost.org …/rasc-dsc.html) redirected and was not fetched, and quasar or galaxy-cluster designations (likely 3C 273, Abell clusters, Leo I etc.) are unverified.
- I did not find RASC terms of use beyond "All rights reserved", nor any public statement on reuse of lists in apps.
- I did not look at the Observer's Handbook's own binocular and double-star lists (e.g. the Handbook's "Deep-Sky Wonders"-style or binocular sections) in this pass.
- The RASC Messier program is Messier's 110, which the app already has, so it was not researched further.

## Other club and society lists (southern, SAC, BAA, Webb, ASSA, RASNZ, Stellafane)

### Takeaway
The strongest southern candidates are the **ASSA Top-100** (100 objects, free on the ASSA site, 86 NGC + 6 IC + 8 others), the **Bennett catalogue** (152 objects, by Jack Bennett, ASSA, 1969/1974) and the **Dunlop 100** (selected by Cozens & White in Sky & Telescope, June 2001). Bennett and Dunlop's own catalogues are historical lists, and their source catalogues are long out of copyright. The "100 best" selection from Dunlop's catalogue is a magazine selection. The BAA runs programmes on Messier, Caldwell and object classes rather than a distinct curated list. I found no RASNZ list.

### Cited Findings
**ASSA (Astronomical Society of Southern Africa)**
- ASSA Top-100: "the 100 best galaxies, star clusters and nebulae visible in the southern skies", all south of the celestial equator. 86 NGC, 6 IC, 8 from other catalogues. Per the fetched table: 39 open clusters, 24 globulars, 15 galaxies, 8 planetary nebulae, 8 bright nebulae, 5 dark nebulae and 1 star cloud. All are visible in binoculars, and more than 45 can be seen in a given month. A General Observer's Certificate is given per 20 objects. The PDF is at assa.saao.ac.za (…/2013/06/assa-top100-deepskyobjects-201305.pdf). The page states no copyright or terms. — [ASSA Top-100](https://assa.saao.ac.za/?p=1105)
- ASSA Deep-Sky Marathons offer an "ASSA Deepsky 100" list (2021), a Lacaille (1755) list of 41 objects and a SkyTools 3 list. Dunlop and Bennett are named as alternatives. The page states no copyright. — [ASSA Deep-Sky Marathons](https://assa.saao.ac.za/?p=8827)
- Bennett Catalogue: 152 objects compiled by South African comet hunter Jack Bennett (5-inch refractor), published 1969 and updated 1974. It includes the Tarantula, Omega Centauri, 47 Tuc, the Sombrero, the Helix, Centaurus A and others, with 26 Messier objects. The Astronomical League runs a Bennett program at 107 (certificate) and 152 (complete) levels. — [ASSA Bennett](https://assa.saao.ac.za/?p=2885); [Astronomical League Bennett Observing Program](https://www.astroleague.org/?p=7224)

**Dunlop 100**
- James Dunlop catalogued 629 southern objects at Parramatta in 1826. Glen Cozens and Graeme White recovered over 300 of them and published "the 100 best objects from Dunlop's list – the Dunlop 100" in Sky & Telescope, June 2001. The list is ordered by Dunlop number (Δ) and declination. Besides NGC/IC, entries include Trumpler 10 and Bernes 157 (dark nebula, CrA). The Prescott Astronomy Club PDF has no copyright notice. — [Dunlop 100 (Prescott AC PDF)](https://prescottastronomyclub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dunlop-100.pdf)
- The SEDS pages also cover Dunlop's full catalogue. — [SEDS Dunlop](https://messier.lamost.org/seds/seds.org/messier/en/xtra/similar/dunlop.html)

**Other southern lists**
- BAM600, John Bambury's Southern Skies list of 600 targets across all types and apertures, was added by the Astronomical League as a supplementary list to its Southern Skies Telescope badge. This was found only via a forum (IceInSpace). — [IceInSpace thread](https://www.iceinspace.com.au/forum/showthread.php?p=1307076)
- Webb Society Deep-Sky Observers' Handbooks (UK) ran 1975–1990. Vol. 7 "The Southern Sky" (1987) is by Steven J. Hynes. — [IceInSpace thread](https://www.iceinspace.com.au/forum/showthread.php?p=1098944)
- Hartung's *Astronomical Objects for Southern Telescopes* was called the most popular southern guide in a 2003 forum post. Cambridge University Press publishes the current edition (front matter, ISBN 9781107015012). — [IceInSpace](https://www.iceinspace.com.au/forum/showthread.php?p=479995); [CUP front matter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/15012/frontmatter/9781107015012_frontmatter.pdf)

**BAA**
- The BAA Deep Sky Section's current programmes are the Messier objects, the Caldwell objects, Variable Nebulae, Planetary Nebulae, Double Stars and Globular Clusters. Previous ones were Local Group Galaxies, Supernova Remnants and Visibility of the Veil Nebula. No counts or downloads are on the page. "Copyright © 2026 British Astronomical Association. All rights reserved." — [BAA Deep Sky observing programmes](https://britastro.org/section_information_/deep-sky-section-overview/observing-programmes); [BAA Caldwell programme](https://britastro.org/node/5556)

**Saguaro Astronomy Club (SAC)**
- SAC has kept the 110 Best of the NGC since 1990 (also sold as a 124-page book). Its menu separately lists a "Best 110 Double Stars", an "Urban List" (objects viewable from Metro Phoenix, with separation and PA for doubles) and "astronomical objects for a public viewing". SAC also publishes a deep-sky database (about 10,000 objects) and a Double Star Database v4.0 (2008, Steve Coe), with downloads on its site. — [SAC Urban List](https://www.saguaroastro.org/the-urban-list); [SAC downloads](https://www.saguaroastro.org/sac-downloads/); [SAC 110 Best book](https://www.saguaroastro.org/book-sacs-110-best-of-the-ngc); [SAC public viewing list](https://www.saguaroastro.org/astronomical-objects-for-a-public-viewing)

### Inferences
- **ASSA Top-100** is the clearest southern counterpart to SAC 110/Finest NGC: free, club-published, 92% NGC/IC, and binocular-level. The 8 "other" entries likely include LMC/SMC, Coalsack and similar named objects, which need hand-added entries. ASSA states no copyright terms, so asking ASSA for permission is easy and low-risk.
- **Bennett 152** is a 1969/1974 club catalogue that is now itself a recognised catalogue (AL program). It has a strong case as "historical catalogue" data, but the selection is still Bennett/ASSA's. Ask ASSA.
- **Dunlop**: the full Dunlop catalogue (1827) is public domain, and the modern identifications are facts. The "100 best" subset is a Sky & Telescope selection (Cozens & White, 2001), which carries more risk than a club list. One alternative is to ship "Dunlop objects identified as NGC/IC" without the 100-best cut.
- **SAC Urban List** fits the city-sky design target directly (Phoenix metro) and is worth inspecting. SAC already has a relationship with the app via SAC 110.
- The BAA, Webb Society and Hartung offerings are either object-class programmes or book handbooks, so they are not good candidates for designation lists.

### Gaps
- I found no RASNZ (Royal Astronomical Society of New Zealand) published deep-sky list.
- I found no Stellafane/Springfield Telescope Makers observing list in this pass.
- I did not check the SAC "Best 110 Double Stars" contents or the SAC database licence. The SAC downloads page needs a direct look for terms.
- The exact 8 non-NGC/IC entries in the ASSA Top-100 were not listed here. The table is on the ASSA page.
- The authoritative Bennett list text (ASSA page or AL PDF) was not fetched.

## Author and book-based lists (O'Meara, French, Burnham, Moore, Hartung)

### Takeaway
Book-based lists, such as O'Meara's *Hidden Treasures* (109 objects) and *Southern Gems* (120), Sue French's *Deep-Sky Wonders* (100 tours by season and month, from her S&T columns), Burnham's and Hartung's handbooks, are original editorial selections inside copyrighted books from Cambridge University Press, Firefly and Sky Publishing. Fan-made lists circulate, but I found no published permission policy. A closed-source commercial app should not ship them without a licence from the publisher.

### Cited Findings
- *Deep-Sky Companions: Hidden Treasures* (Cambridge University Press) presents an original selection of 109 objects, with front matter "© S. J. O'Meara 2007 … This publication is in copyright." Fan-made PDF lists exist (e.g. catchersofthelight.com). — [CUP front matter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/37040/frontmatter/9780521837040_frontmatter.htm); [Hidden Treasures list (fan PDF)](https://catchersofthelight.com/Downloads/Hidden-Treasures-List.pdf)
- O'Meara's *Southern Gems* presents "a fresh list of 120 deep-sky objects" for southern observers. — [IceInSpace thread](https://www.iceinspace.com.au/forum/showthread.php?p=1307076)
- Sue French's *Deep-Sky Wonders* (Firefly, 2012, 320 pp.) is organised by season and month into 100 tours collected from her Sky & Telescope columns. Her earlier *Celestial Sampler* came from the same column. I found no SkySafari list based on it. — [Peribo listing](https://peribo.com.au/product/deep-sky-wonders/); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/deep-sky-wonders-tour-universe-sky-and-telescopes-sue-french-sue-french)
- SkySafari supports importing and exporting user `.skylist` files. That is how community lists for books circulate (user-made, not publisher-licensed). — [SkySafari observing lists help](https://userguide.skysafariastronomy.com/app-specific/observe-help/observing-lists-help)

### Inferences
- These lists are the highest-risk category. The selection is the author's creative output and the book's commercial value, and publishers (CUP, Firefly, AAS/Sky & Telescope) are commercial rights holders. Shipping "Hidden Treasures 109" as a named list is reproducing the selection, even without any descriptions. Do it only with a licence.
- Patrick Moore's lists beyond Caldwell: I found nothing that warrants a list. Caldwell is already included.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source on whether SkySafari or SkyTools licensed O'Meara, French or Burnham lists. Their in-app lists were not verified.
- *Burnham's Celestial Handbook* selection: not researched, and no sources were fetched. Burnham is a three-volume Dover handbook covering thousands of objects, so it is less a "list" than a reference.
- Sky Atlas 2000 / Pocket Sky Atlas observing lists, and "Mag 6"/"Best of" lists: not researched in this pass.

## Copyright and licence: can a closed-source app include just designations?

### Takeaway
Under US law (Feist v. Rural, 1991), facts such as designations and coordinates are not protected, but an original *selection or arrangement* can be, and the creativity threshold is "extremely modest". In the EU, a separate 15-year sui generis database right protects a substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting contents, against extraction of all or a substantial part. A curated "best N" list of designations is the kind of thing that may carry selection copyright. Club lists with "All rights reserved" are not licensed for reuse. Get permission.

### Cited Findings
- Feist v. Rural (1991): originality requires independent creation plus a modicum of creativity, and the creativity bar is "extremely modest". A white-pages directory failed. — [IT Law Wiki: database protection](https://itlaw.fandom.com/wiki/Copyright_protection_of_databases)
- A compilation of public-domain facts can still be protected "if it features creativity in selection or arrangement", and selection criteria need not be obvious to involve judgment. — [Wikipedia: Copyright in lists](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Wikipedia:CIL)
- EU Directive 96/9/EC database right: arises automatically, protects against extracting or reusing all or a substantial part, including repeated extraction of insubstantial parts, and lasts 15 years, renewed by substantial modification. It is independent of copyright in selection and arrangement. — [Wikipedia: Database right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database_right)
- Catalogue producers do assert compilation copyright (e.g. USNO for USNO-B). — [USNO-B copyright notice](https://padc-web.obspm.fr/perso/ivan/usnob/Copyright)

### Inferences
- **Risk ranking** (low to high):
  1. Historical catalogues as whole catalogues (Dunlop 1827, Lacaille 1755, Bennett as a catalogue, Messier), where there is little or no selection on the app's side.
  2. Club lists whose owners state no terms (ASSA Top-100, Dunlop 100 handouts): ask, and permission is likely.
  3. Club lists with "All rights reserved" (all RASC programs, BAA, SAC): ask the club in writing, and credit it.
  4. Book and magazine selections (O'Meara, French, Burnham, Hartung, S&T's Dunlop 100): need a publisher licence.
- A list of designations is small. EU database-right arguments are weak for, say, 100 rows unless the whole list is taken, but the whole list is exactly what an app would ship. So treat "whole list" as substantial.
- Naming a list after its source (e.g. "Deep-Sky Gems") also implies endorsement or affiliation, a trademark-like concern. Get permission or rename it neutrally.

### Gaps
- No court case on astronomy observing lists specifically was found.
- *British Horseracing Board v William Hill* (database right does not cover the investment in *creating* data) was not confirmed from a fetched source.

## Designations needing data beyond OpenNGC, Messier, Caldwell, WDS bright doubles and named stars

### Takeaway
Most candidate lists are 85–100% NGC/IC. The extras are a small, predictable set: Melotte, Collinder, Trumpler, Tombaugh and Palomar clusters, Barnard and Bernes dark nebulae, Jones 1, UGC/Arp galaxies, quasars, Abell clusters, Levy asterisms, Kemble's Cascade, and the LMC/SMC-type southern named objects. These can live in a small hand-made "extra targets" file (the repo already has an untracked `src/app/core/extra-targets.ts`).

### Cited Findings
- EtU: Melotte 20, Melotte 25, Melotte 111, Collinder 399, Kemble's Cascade (asterism). Doubles by Bayer/Flamsteed name, and Mira/Algol. — [EtU workbook PDF](https://rasc.ca/sites/default/files/EtUworkbookComplete%2002%2013%202020%20for%20web.pdf)
- Deep-Sky Gems: UGC 5373, UGC 6253, Arp 321, Tombaugh 5, Q0957+561 A/B, Levy 384, Levy 401, Levy 403, and IC 2194/2196/2197 as a group. — [DSG PDF](https://www.rasc.ca/sites/default/files/Deep-Sky_Gems_Final_221107.pdf)
- Deep-Sky Challenge: Palomar 11, Jones 1, Barnard 33, Barnard 72, Barnard's Loop, plus galaxy clusters and quasars (designations unverified). — [RASC DSC](https://www.rasc.ca/node/47252)
- Dunlop 100: Trumpler 10 and Bernes 157 among non-NGC entries. — [Dunlop 100 PDF](https://prescottastronomyclub.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dunlop-100.pdf)
- ASSA Top-100: 8 entries from catalogues other than NGC/IC. — [ASSA Top-100](https://assa.saao.ac.za/?p=1105)

### Inferences
- Coordinates for these extras are facts and can be typed in from a permissive source (e.g. SIMBAD/VizieR positions, with attribution) without touching any GPL catalogue.
- Levy asterisms have no external catalogue entry. Their positions would come from the DSG list itself, which is one more reason to get RASC permission.
- RASC Double Stars 110 and the SAC doubles need a double-star source keyed by discoverer code (STF, STT, etc.). Check that WDS coverage in the app goes beyond "bright doubles".

### Gaps
- Complete non-NGC breakdowns for the DSC, ASSA Top-100 and Bennett were not extracted.
